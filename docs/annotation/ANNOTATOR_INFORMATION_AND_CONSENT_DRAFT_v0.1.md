## 1. Purpose

This research examines how social context affects the appropriate use
of the Bangla address registers TUI, TUMI, and APNI in Romanized Bangla
messages.

## 2. What Participation Involves

The participant may be asked to:

- read Romanized Bangla messages and social-context descriptions;
- complete a 12-item qualification exercise;
- identify the most socially appropriate address register;
- optionally identify one secondary acceptable register;
- record confidence and relevant reason codes; and
- provide a short note when clarification is necessary.

Qualified annotators may later be invited to annotate the 45-item pilot.

## 3. Voluntary Participation

Participation is voluntary.

The participant may decline to participate or stop before submitting
the completed responses without penalty.

The researcher must explain any department-specific withdrawal deadline
before consent is collected.

## 4. Possible Risks

The task presents no expected physical risk.

Some scenarios may involve social hierarchy, age, family relationships,
anger, or culturally variable forms of address. A participant may skip
the study if these judgments cause discomfort.

## 5. Confidentiality

The research dataset will identify annotators using anonymous codes such
as `A1` and `A2`.

Names, telephone numbers, email addresses, signatures, and other direct
identifiers will not be placed in the public dataset.

Consent records and raw annotation files will be stored privately and
separately from public research outputs.

## 6. Independent Annotation

Before submitting responses, participants must not:

- inspect another annotator's answers;
- discuss individual pilot items with another annotator;
- use ChatGPT or another AI system to select labels;
- search online for answers to individual items; or
- inspect the private reference key or designer expectations.

Participants may ask the researcher questions about the written
procedure. The researcher must not recommend a label for a specific
pilot item.

## 7. Use of Results

Anonymized annotation results and aggregate agreement statistics may be
used in:

- the undergraduate thesis;
- academic presentations;
- research reports;
- a public dataset or repository; and
- possible later academic publications.

Direct personal identifiers will not be included in those outputs.
